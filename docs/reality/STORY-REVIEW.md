# Five-chapter explanation review

Authored content: `src/situations/story.js`, exported as `TONGA_STORY`. This is an explanation layer over `tongaSituation`, not a separate factual dataset. Each chapter supplies a camera destination, historical display time, visual category and record ID for source interrogation. `sourceRecordId` may refer to a retrospective account published after the displayed date; opening a source must preserve its publication date.

## Editorial decisions

The opening explains the subject before asking the viewer to operate controls. Every chapter answers a question and ends in one takeaway. Caveats are placed where they change the conclusion: geography limits in disruption, chronology in evidence, incomplete recovery at the end. The explanatory text avoids fake precision, percentages, sample charts and claims of uninterrupted national zero traffic.

The chapter times are presentation anchors. They do not assert the exact time of cable failure, satellite activation or physical repair completion. The emergency chapter uses the date by which ITU documented assistance. The recovery chapter uses the actual operator announcement timestamp; its traffic onset remains approximate.

The opening and conclusion are original synthesis, not new evidence. The conclusion identifies physical dependence and uneven recovery without claiming the sources prove a complete causal chain. Source popups must retain spatial context descriptions, exact-versus-approximate time metadata and source publication dates.

## Acceptance questions for visual review

- Can a first-time viewer explain the cable’s role after chapter one without reading a source popup?
- Does moving between chapters visibly change the represented state rather than only replacing text?
- Is the 03:00 → 04:14 → 05:30 sequence understandable without specialist terminology?
- Is emergency access clearly different from broad service recovery?
- Does the ending make incomplete domestic recovery visible, rather than merely hiding that detail in a citation?
- Can the viewer open the source for the claim currently being explained and return to the same chapter?

This document is an editorial/source audit, not proof of completed visual acceptance. That requires the implemented chapter states to be rendered and inspected.
